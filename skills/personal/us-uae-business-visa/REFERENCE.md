# Reference — U.S. and UAE Business Visa Skill

This file records the official sources and the current facts that were verified when the skill was created. Re-check the official pages before every filing because visa rules and service requirements can change.

## Council for Education (CED) — verified IRS status

Source: IRS Letter 947 dated January 2, 2025.

Verified facts:

- Legal name: Council for Education.
- EIN: 82-3295336.
- Federal tax-exempt status: exempt under IRC §501(c)(3).
- Public-charity classification: IRC §509(a)(2).
- Effective date of exemption: September 20, 2024.
- Contribution deductibility: yes; the IRS letter states that donors can deduct contributions to CED under IRC §170.

Required wording when CED status is material:

**Council for Education (CED), a 501(c)(3) tax-exempt public charity under IRC §509(a)(2), EIN 82-3295336, with exemption effective September 20, 2024. Contributions to CED are deductible under IRC §170.**

### Mandatory separation rule — charitable contributions vs. securities investments

- Do not conflate a charitable contribution to CED with an investment made under a PPM, Regulation D, Regulation S, Revenue Participation Interest, or other securities offering.
- CED's §501(c)(3) status and §170 contribution deductibility do not make a securities purchase tax-deductible.
- A securities investment should be described and documented as an investment, not as a charitable donation, unless qualified U.S. tax and securities counsel specifically determine otherwise for a particular transaction.
- Keep charitable fundraising, securities offering, visa, banking, DIFC-incorporation, and source-of-funds narratives separate unless a specific factual relationship must be disclosed.
- Do not imply that the IRS determination letter approves, validates, guarantees, or endorses an investment, offering, visa application, DIFC entity, bank application, or financial product.
- If current exempt status is material to a filing or transaction, verify that no later IRS action has superseded Letter 947.

### Verified current points

- A foreign national traveling temporarily to attend a professional, scientific, educational, or business conference may fit B-1 visitor activity if otherwise eligible.
- A B visitor applicant completes DS-160 and prints the confirmation/barcode page.
- The current non-petition-based B visa application fee is USD 185; verify before payment.
- Passport validity is generally at least six months beyond the intended U.S. stay unless a country-specific exception applies.
- Interviews are generally required, subject to limited waiver rules. A first-time B applicant should plan for an in-person interview unless the post confirms otherwise.
- Current State Department policy says NIV applicants should schedule in their country of nationality or residence.
- For Pakistani nationals, the current reciprocity schedule lists B-1, B-2, and B-1/B-2 as multiple-entry, 60-month validity, with no separate reciprocity issuance fee. This is visa validity, not the length of each admitted stay.
- There is no published fixed minimum bank balance for a B-1/B-2 application. Funding is evaluated in context.
- A conference invitation supports purpose but does not replace the applicant's own eligibility, temporary intent, funding evidence, and ties outside the United States.

### Recommended evidence architecture

**Mandatory/core**
- Passport
- DS-160 confirmation
- Fee receipt where required
- Interview appointment confirmation
- Photograph if required by the post or if upload fails

**Purpose**
- Conference invitation
- Registration confirmation
- Agenda
- Venue and dates
- Short itinerary

**Funding**
- Bank evidence appropriate to the facts
- Employer/organization sponsorship letter when genuine
- Evidence of who pays airfare, lodging, and expenses

**Return ties**
- Employment/business/academic commitments
- Residence and family obligations where relevant
- Approved leave and expected return date
- Other genuine social or economic ties

### Critical routing rule

Do not combine GDRFA and ICP document lists as though they are one application. First determine which authority/channel and visa duration the applicant will actually use.

### Dubai/GDRFA — verified current points

For the Dubai service titled "Issuing a visit visa for the purpose of exploring business opportunities":

- It is described as a visit visa for researching/exploring business opportunities.
- The service states that no UAE sponsor/host is required.
- The published requirements currently list:
  - color personal photograph;
  - passport copy, with at least six months' validity;
  - proof of investment.
- Verify current fees, entry validity, duration, and filing-channel details directly on GDRFA before submission.

### Federal ICP 90-day route — verified current points

The 90-day single-entry business-establishment exploration service currently states:

- no UAE host is required;
- entry visa is valid for 60 days from issuance for entry into the UAE;
- UAE-valid health insurance is required;
- passport must be valid for at least six months;
- required documents include passport, personal photo, initial approval from relevant authorities for business establishment, a stamped bank statement covering at least six months, and a bachelor's degree or equivalent;
- a financial guarantee is required; current published amount is AED 1,000;
- the published service does not state a substitute fixed bank-balance amount for the six-month bank statement.

ICP also states that a person exploring business establishment should demonstrate financial capability appropriate to the proposed activity, or be engaged in that activity through an existing business outside the UAE, or be a practicing professional in the field.

### DIFC incorporation context — verified current points

For a DIFC private company, the current DIFC handbook describes a five-step setup sequence:

1. Submit "Register with DIFC" through the DIFC Portal.
2. Receive initial approval after review.
3. Complete the registered-address/lease step as applicable.
4. Pay applicable fees and electronically sign constitutional documents.
5. Obtain the licence and Certificate of Incorporation.

The current private-company handbook states that a private company must have at least one shareholder, at least one director, and issued share capital greater than zero. Final legal form and regulatory classification must be determined from the intended activities.

### Recommended UAE business-purpose file

- UAE law-firm engagement letter
- Formal appointment confirmation stating date, time, place, counsel contact, and business-establishment purpose
- Proposed entity/jurisdiction and activity memorandum
- Ownership/governance information
- Corporate authorization for the traveler to attend/coordinate incorporation work
- Funding evidence for legal fees, incorporation, travel, and accommodation
- DIFC initial approval or other business-establishment approval if required by the selected visa route
- Return/onward travel and accommodation evidence
- UAE-valid insurance where required

## Reasoning rules for financial evidence

- Never claim that USD 4,000, USD 10,000, USD 15,000, or any other figure is a general minimum for the UAE business-opportunity visa unless the selected official service expressly publishes that number.
- Do not substitute a balance certificate for a six-month statement when the selected service expressly requires the statement.
- A balance certificate can be supplemental evidence when permitted, but it should not be presented as a replacement for an express documentary requirement.
- Flag large recent deposits and ask for legitimate source-of-funds documentation rather than suggesting cosmetic account funding.

## Consistency audit

Before filing either visa case, compare all forms and letters for:

- full legal name and passport details;
- nationality and residence;
- job title and employer/organization;
- CED status wording when material;
- whether a payment is a charitable contribution or a securities investment;
- ownership statements;
- trip dates and length;
- destination and venue/office;
- purpose of travel;
- source of travel funds;
- prior travel and visa history;
- whether the applicant is seeking employment or only conducting permitted temporary business activity.
